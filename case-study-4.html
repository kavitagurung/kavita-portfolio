CASE FILE 02 / RELEASE CONFIDENCE

AI Quality Gates for CI/CD

Turning automated execution results into informed release-confidence decisions.

Overview

A workflow that brings automated execution results into API-first CI/CD delivery decisions.

The problem

Binary pass/fail results do not always provide enough context for enterprise release decisions.

My role

Workflow definition, prioritisation, policy design, stakeholder alignment, and enterprise-readiness planning.

Key product decisions

01 / CONTEXT

Separate critical failures from warnings.

Context: Automated executions can contain failures with very different release implications.

Decision: Define critical, warning, and pass result policies.

Why it mattered: Teams can make an informed release decision rather than react to an undifferentiated result total.

02 / TRACEABILITY

Make the decision explainable.

Context: Enterprise teams need to understand why a release was blocked or permitted.

Decision: Prioritise result summaries and auditable policy outcomes.

Why it mattered: Release confidence is stronger when the reasoning is visible to the people accountable for it.

03 / INTEGRATION

Meet delivery in its existing flow.

Context: Quality feedback is most useful when it arrives where delivery decisions happen.

Decision: Frame execution around API-first CI/CD workflows.

Why it mattered: The capability supports operational delivery rather than becoming a separate reporting destination.

Evidence and outcomes

  • Defined a release-confidence model that accommodates pass-with-warning outcomes alongside release-blocking failures.
  • Used customer and enterprise workflow context to inform prioritisation and readiness decisions.
  • Connected quality gates with related testing, governance, and release-management considerations.

What I learned

Release confidence is not a score. It is a decision made with the right policy, evidence, and operational context.
